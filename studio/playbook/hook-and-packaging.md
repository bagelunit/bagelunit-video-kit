# The hook and the packaging of a long video

Read at step 4 (the map) and steps 5, 11 and 12 (script, thumbnail, title). A long video is won or lost twice: on the page of thumbnails, where the title and thumbnail make a promise, and in the first 30 seconds, where the video proves it will keep that promise. Everything below is in service of those two moments.

## 1. Packaging comes first

Decide the title and the thumbnail at the map, before a word of the script is written: if the promise cannot be said in a title and shown in a thumbnail, the video does not have one yet.

- **One promise.** The title, the thumbnail and the first sentence all point at the same thing. A viewer who clicked for X hears X within the first ten seconds.
- **The title** (100 characters at most, about 60 show): a specific outcome, question or surprise, in plain words. Numbers only from research.md. No clickbait the video does not pay off; no "in this video".
- **The thumbnail** adds what the title does not say. One subject, 2 to 5 words at most, one word in the accent colour (`video thumbnail --text "WORDS *ONE*"`). It has to read at 320 px wide (the size on a phone): look at it small.
- **Offer options.** The report at step 4 gives three title options and two or three thumbnail ideas (frame plus words), with the one recommended first. Make two or three versions later (`--name a`, `--name b`) for YouTube's thumbnail test, which the person starts in YouTube Studio.

## 2. The first 30 seconds: the triple hook

The kit's hooks follow BagelUnit's triple hook (the Reel Kit's `docs/hook-and-review-playbook.md`, section 0), stretched to the pace of a long video:

1. **Context, at once (0 to 5 s).** Say what the video is about in the first sentence, show it in the first scene and, where it fits, write it on screen. No greeting, no logo, no "welcome back".
2. **The lean (5 to 15 s).** Make the viewer curious in one direction: a number from research.md, a pain they have, a benefit they want, or something surprising.
3. **The contrarian snapback (15 to 25 s).** Pull them another way: the thing everyone believes is not the whole story, and this video shows what is. It must be true, and the video pays it off.
4. **The road ahead (25 to 30 s).** One sentence of what they will get, so they know the payoff is coming ("by the end you will know ..."). Then go.

**The read-back test.** Read the first 30 seconds of script.txt on their own: is the context clear at once, is there a lean, does it then snap them in a direction they did not expect, and does it say what they get? Four yeses or rewrite it. Score the opening out of 100 against these four and the promise of the title; below 90, rewrite and score again (never fudge the number).

## 3. Keeping them

- **Open a loop, close it later.** Set up a question early that the last chapter answers.
- **A new picture every 5 to 15 seconds**, and something that changes the pace every 30 to 60 seconds: a chapter card, a big number, a map, a story, a recap. Spread the templates.
- **Each chapter opens with a small hook** (context and lean in one or two sentences), not "next, let's talk about".
- **The payoff is the ending.** End on it and the call to action; no "that is all for today", no long goodbye. Leave 10 to 20 seconds at the end for YouTube's end screen (a calm last scene with nothing important in it).
