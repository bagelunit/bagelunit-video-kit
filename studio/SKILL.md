# BagelUnit Video Kit

Long and standard videos at 1920x1080, made scene by scene. The rule that splits this kit from the Reel Kit is the picture shape, not the length: wide here, vertical there.

All commands are `node <this skill's folder>/tools/video.mjs <command>` (the folder is `~/.claude/skills/bagelunit-video` once installed; below it is written `video`). `video doctor` says what is installed.

## A video folder

```
video.json     {"style": "<id>", "title": "...", "seed": 1, "voice": {...optional}, "voice_speed": 1, "sfx": "chapters", "publish_on": "YYYY-MM-DD", "channel": "default", "privacy": "private"}
               optional: "music": "file", "music_volume": 0.18, "music_fade": [3, 8], "end_card": 15, "thumbnail": {"plain": true},
               licensed (any source, Uppbeat suggested; by id from brand/licensed/assets.json): "look": {"lut": "<id>", "strength": 1},
               "music": [{"asset": "<id>", "from": "<scene id or seconds>", "fade": 2, "gain_db": -18}]
               "language": "en", "tags": ["..."], "publish_at": "2026-11-17T18:00:00-05:00", "description_sources": false
research.md    what the video may claim, each fact with its source (video new writes the empty page)
outline.md     the map: promise, hook, chapters, one line per scene, payoff, and the music plan table (step 4)
script-brief.md the script plan (/script-plan): the one viewer, the promise, each chapter's hook, point and minutes
script.txt     the narration, paragraphs between ideas (every line of it is voiced: no notes in it)
assets/blender/ the owner's own Blender renders for this video (kept out of git; a scene names them with "file")
scenes.json    {"scenes": [{"chapter": "optional title", "template": "<id>", "props": {...}, "narration": "exact words from script.txt", "pause": 4}]}
audio/vo.mp3   the voice (video voice), build/words.json the clock of every word, build/voice_source.json who spoke (see step 8)
approvals.md   the record of each fact check, script review, voice and approval (the kit writes it; earlier lines are never changed)
build/         scriptcheck.md (video script), coldread.md (/script-coldread), pace.json (video pace), mix.json (video mixcheck), and the rest the kit makes
out/           video.mp4, chapters.txt, thumbnail.jpg, youtube.txt (title on line 1, description after), pinned-comment.txt,
               captions.<lang>.srt (video captions)
```

## In a channel folder

A channel folder (made by `/video-setup`; it has `brand/brand.json` and a `videos/` folder) is one YouTube channel. Videos live in `videos/<name>`: `video new <name> --style <id>` puts them there. Each video then belongs to the channel:

- **Read first:** `brand/brand.json` (name, `niche`, `youtube`, `voice`, `style`, `cta`), `brand/voice.md` (how the channel talks) and `brand/facts.md` (cleared facts and the "Never say" list). Pick the style from `style.usual` when it fits; never one in `style.never`. A `CLAUDE.local.md` there holds the owner's own rules for that channel.
- **Voice:** the voice needs no outside tool. With no ElevenLabs key the free built-in voice speaks (this computer's own speech engine, basic quality; it needs the Reel Kit 1.3.0 or newer). ElevenLabs is only suggested for a natural voice (`video suggest elevenlabs`); the channel's ElevenLabs voice and `voice.speed` are used unless video.json sets its own, and `brand.json` `voice.provider` can be `command` (your own text-to-speech command). Spell tricky names in script.txt the way `voice.pronunciation` says (on screen they keep their real spelling).
- **Writing:** `brand/jargon.md` lists words the voice must explain in plain words the first time it says them (`video script` checks it); `brand.json` `writing` holds `human_score_min` (90 or more) and `banned_phrases` for the script's human score.
- **Facts:** a cleared fact in `brand/facts.md` counts as a source in the fact check; anything on the never-say lists (`voice.never_say`, and "Never say" in facts.md) fails it.
- **Call to action:** the last scene and the description end on `cta.line`.
- **YouTube:** the sign-in is the channel's own (saved under `slug`), and uploads use `youtube.privacy` (private or unlisted) unless a video says otherwise.
- **Reels:** `reels_folder` points at the channel's Reel Kit brand folder, where `video reels` makes reels.

A Reel Kit brand folder works the same way (videos go in its `videos/`, next to `reels/`). Outside any of these the video stands alone and none of this applies.

## The order of work

**Every question to the person is asked with the AskUserQuestion tool** (a selectable block: two to four options, the recommended one first, "Other" for typing), never as plain text in the chat. That includes every go-ahead below.

**Outside tools are only suggested.** The kit works on pure code rendering. `video suggest` lists optional tools (a voice service, licensed music, Blender, a generated-video tool, Canva or Adobe), says what each adds with an example and which are connected; `/suggested-tools` in a channel folder asks which the person has and records the answers (`tools` in brand.json). A one-line `Tip:` may appear on stderr at `video new`, `video thumbnail`, `video render` and `video voice` (once per tool per 7 days; `--no-tips`, `BAGELUNIT_NO_TIPS=1` or `tools.tips=false` turn it off). Never push a tool, never name a price, never require one.

1. **Choose the style.** `video styles` lists 20 (the newest, `deep-zoom`, is a 3D science documentary look); `video styles <id>` lists its templates. Pick the one that fits the kind of video (a history story, a science explainer, a data breakdown, a children's story) and be ready to say why in the report. Read that style's `styles/<id>/SKILL.md` (art direction), `templates.json` (every template's props) and, for custom scenes, `docs.md`. One style per video. When the topic does not decide it, ask with AskUserQuestion: the two or three best-fitting styles as options (the recommended one first, each with a short reason).
2. **Start the folder.** `video new <folder> --style <id> --title "..."` writes a sample plan from the style's first three templates (replace it) and an empty `research.md`.
3. **Research (research.md).** Search the web and read the pages themselves, not the search snippets. Prefer the people who know first hand: official bodies, the studies, the organisation's own figures. Every number, date and named claim needs a source, two that agree for anything surprising; note when each was published. Fill in `research.md`: the question, what was found (one fact per line with its link), what is not sure or disputed, and the sources. Never list a source you did not read. When it is done, delete the "not researched yet" line: `video voice` refuses until the page has at least two sources. A video with nothing to check (a made-up story) says why in video.json: `"no_research": "a bedtime story, nothing to check"`.
4. **Report and map, then stop.** Write `outline.md` as the map from start to end: the promise in one sentence, the hook, the chapters, a line per scene (its one point and the fact behind it), the payoff in the last chapter. Open on the hook, never a greeting or "in this video". Alternate explanation, example, data, story and recap so the picture keeps changing. Only claims that are in research.md; nothing invented. No real people, other companies' characters or logos: invent names. Then give the person a report in the chat:
   - **What the research found:** the 5 to 10 facts the video rests on, each with its source, and what was not sure or disputed and how the video treats it (left out, or said as uncertain).
   - **The style** and why it fits.
   - **The map:** hook, chapters with their scenes in one line each, payoff, and the estimated length (planned words / 150 = minutes).
   - **The packaging:** three title options and two or three thumbnail ideas (a frame and 2 to 5 words), the recommended one first (`playbook/hook-and-packaging.md` section 1).
   - **The music plan** (long videos): a table in `outline.md` under a heading that says "Music plan", one row per chapter (mood, tempo, energy, asset), from the starter `brand/music-plan.md`; `video musicplan <folder>` checks it later (step 10).
   - **Parts, if it runs long** (see "Long videos" below).
   - **What the voice will cost:** about the script's length in characters (roughly 900 a minute).
   `/script-plan` does this planning in order (the one viewer, the promise, each chapter's small hook, point and minutes, the payoff and call to action, the estimated length, the opening 30 seconds written and scored) and writes `script-brief.md` and the chapter plan in `outline.md`; it writes no script. Then ask with AskUserQuestion: "Go ahead with this map (Recommended)", "Change the map", "Stop here" (and, for the packaging, a second question with the three title options). Nothing past this step (script, voice, render) happens until they choose to go ahead. On their go-ahead, record it: `video approve <folder> map --note "<what they said>"`. If the map or the research changes later, show the person what changed and approve again; the voice checks.
5. **Script (script.txt).** Follow the approved map. The first 30 seconds are the triple hook: context at once, the lean, the contrarian snapback, then what the viewer will get (`playbook/hook-and-packaging.md` section 2); run the read-back test and score the opening, 90 or more or rewrite it. Each chapter opens with a small hook; the video ends on the payoff and the call to action, with a calm last 10 to 20 seconds for YouTube's end screen. Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea per sentence, numbers said the way they are spoken. About 150 words a minute. A blank line between ideas. No filler openings or closings. Every line of script.txt is voiced, so it holds the spoken words only (no notes, headings or stage directions).
   **The script flow, in this order:** (a) `video script <folder> [--minutes N]` is the script check (no model, no credits): length against the plan, the opening, chapter hooks, the ending, long sentences, fragments, numbers and names, lists, jargon from `brand/jargon.md`, filler, repeated openings, links; it writes `build/scriptcheck.md` and exits 1 on any error. `--human` prints the human score (how unlike stock AI prose it reads, with the channel's `writing.human_score_min` and `banned_phrases`; 90 or more). (b) `/script-coldread`: a fresh agent that sees only the spoken words says what the video is about, what it promised, where it got confused or would stop watching; it writes `build/coldread.md`; fix what it found and check again. (c) the fact check (step 7). (d) `/script-score`: a fresh context scores the script out of 100 against a written rubric; below 90 it fixes the weakest part and scores again; at 90 or more it records the line: `video script <folder> --record --score NN [--notes "weakest: pace"]`, which writes `Script review: meets the bar (NN/100; ...) [script <fingerprint>]` to `approvals.md`. The fingerprint is of the words (`video script <folder> --hash`): change one word of script.txt and the review no longer fits, so score and record again. `video script <folder> --status` says whether the gate before the voice is open.
6. **Scenes (scenes.json).** One scene per idea, 5 to 15 seconds of narration (the planner flags under 2 s and over 16 s). `narration` is the exact words of script.txt the scene covers, in order, and every word of the script belongs to exactly one scene: times come from the voice's word clock, never from typed numbers. Spread templates; never the same one many times in a row. Mark the first scene of each chapter with `"chapter": "Short title"` (2 to 6 words, specific): these become YouTube chapters (at least 3, the first at 0:00, 10 s apart or more). **A stretch with no voice** (a brand intro, a breath before a reveal): `"pause": 4` on a scene puts that many seconds of silence in the voice at the start of the scene, before its first word; the scene owns the silence. A scene with a pause and no narration is all silence (a `"code"` intro ident, for example). Never splice silence into audio/vo.mp3 or shift build/words.json by hand. For a silent card after the voice ends, use `"end_card"` (step 10) instead.
7. **Fact check.** `video facts <folder>` reads every number in the script and on screen (spoken or written: "sixty four percent" matches "64%") and fails any that is not in research.md (or the brand's facts.md); a rounded number is a warning to say "about"; absolute words (only, never, biggest) are listed, because they need a source that says exactly that; the brand's never-say list fails. Fix every error. Then re-read the sources for each line in the worksheet `build/factcheck.md`, and record the verdict: `video facts <folder> record --result PASS` (refused while there are errors). Changing the script, the scenes or the research afterwards makes it out of date.
8. **Voice.** `video voice <folder>` refuses, in this order, until the research is done, the map is approved, the fact check is a current PASS and the script review is recorded for these exact words (step 5; `video revoice` the same). Only the owner overrides the review, never the fact check: `--skip-script-review "reason"` on `voice` or `revoice` records `Script review: SKIPPED by the owner ...` with the reason and the script's fingerprint in `approvals.md`, and a later change to the script cancels it. **Which voice speaks:** it says so before it starts. With an ElevenLabs key saved, ElevenLabs (natural); with no key, the free built-in voice (basic quality, needs the Reel Kit 1.3.0 or newer; `video doctor` shows which one will speak); `--provider local|elevenlabs|command` chooses one for this run (`command` is your own text-to-speech command from brand.json `voice`). The credit check (the ElevenLabs characters left for the chunks not voiced yet) only applies to ElevenLabs: for the built-in voice and your own command it prints "not needed". When there are not enough characters it stops: say so and ask with AskUserQuestion what to do: "Shorten the script", "Split into parts", "Top up or wait for the reset". Then it speaks script.txt in chunks with the Reel Kit's voice tool (a chunk is only paid for once; a re-run after a failure pays only for chunks still missing) and joins them (with each scene's `"pause"` spliced in; a word that would run over a splice is cut at it), at natural pace (`"voice_speed"` in video.json changes it, 0.7 to 1.3; reels are sped up, videos are not; with an up-to-date Reel Kit engine a new speed re-cuts the take for free, an older engine voices it again, so then set it before the first voice). Changing a `"pause"` later needs only `video voice` again: nothing is voiced again, the chunks are reused (the plan and the render say when the voice and scenes.json disagree). After joining it records who spoke (`build/voice_source.json`, tied to the audio, and a line under "Voice" in `approvals.md`; the build, QA and the dashboard read it). If the word times are only **estimated** (Whisper is not installed), it warns that captions and cuts may drift and says how to install Whisper (nothing is voiced again after); `video captions` repeats the warning. After joining it checks the word clock against the audio, chunk by chunk: a **WARNING** that the words run past the audio means captions and cuts will be late there; listen, and voice that chunk again (delete its `build/voice/NNN` folder; only it is paid for). **One line again:** to change a single line after the voice is made (a word, a fix, a platform's version), do not voice it alone (a line voiced alone comes out at a different pace). `video revoice <folder> --line "<the words as said>" --to "<new words>"` is a dry run that shows the lead-in (the sentence before the line, voiced with it for the pace, then cut off) and the characters it would send; `--apply` voices it (once: the same request is reused for free) and splices it into a copy, `audio/vo-revoiced.mp3`, which `--use` makes the voice (the old one is kept as `audio/vo-before-revoice.mp3`; change script.txt too, as `video voice` would join the chunks again). With `--vertical` it changes only the vertical voice (`audio/vo-vertical.mp3`, `build/words-vertical.json`, `build/pauses-vertical.json`), e.g. "subscribe" to "follow" for Instagram. It prints words per second for the new line, the old line and the episode, and warns past 20%: listen when it does. Nothing can speak (no key and no speech engine): `video stand-in <folder>` makes a tone and fake word times so everything else can be built and checked (it needs no approval); the render warns loudly while the stand-in is in use, and QA and the upload refuse it. The built-in voice counts as a real voice.
9. **Plan and check.** `video plan <folder>` shows each scene's time and what was found in the word clock. `video check <folder>` draws every scene, reports errors, anything outside the title-safe area (5% margin), and whether each scene seeks to the same picture every time, with stills in `build/scenes/check/`. Look at the stills. Fix, then check again. `video pace <folder>` asks whether the picture keeps changing: a new picture every 4 to 6 seconds in a busy style (a calm style such as `deep-zoom` has its own band), no scene over 8 s without a change, the same template not three times in a row, every chapter varied; PASS, WARN and FAIL lines, and `build/pace.json` (exit 1 on a FAIL). Fix with more scenes or other templates, not by typing times.
10. **Render.** `video render <folder>` renders only changed scenes (a scene whose words and length did not change keeps its render even when it moved in time, for example after a pause was added before it; only its clip's sound is made again), joins them without re-encoding the picture, mixes the voice, the `music` from video.json (ducked under speech) and the sound effects at -14 LUFS, and writes `out/video.mp4` and `out/chapters.txt`. `--scene n` renders one scene; `--force` renders all. Sound effects are made in code from the Reel Kit's sound bank (nothing to license): `"sfx": "chapters"` (default, a whoosh as each chapter starts), `"scenes"` (also a soft swoosh on every cut) or `"off"`; a scene can add its own with `"sfx": "pop"` or `[{"at": 1.2, "name": "pop"}]` (whoosh swoosh pop click tick thud impact boom shimmer riser type cash stamp). `"sfx_gain"` (dB, default 9) sets how far they rise; keep them under the voice, they should sit in the pauses. The music is cut to the video's length with `"music_fade": [in, out]` seconds of fade (without it: no fade-in and a 3 s fade-out, never a hard cut); a track shorter than the video loops. **End card:** `"end_card": 15` runs the video that many seconds past the voice for YouTube's end screen (5 to 20 s); the last scene holds it, so make the last scene the card (a scene with no narration). QA allows the extra length and a still end card.
    **Music plan check:** `video musicplan <folder>` checks the music plan table in `outline.md` against the chapters in scenes.json, video.json `"music"` and the register: an asset not in the register, a planned asset no `"music"` entry starts, a chapter with no row or no music of its own, music that changes mid-chapter, an abrupt switch (a fade under 1 s where the mood changes). FAIL, WARN and INFO lines; exit 1 on a FAIL.
    **Licensed material** (any source you have rights to, Uppbeat is what we use and suggest; YouTube only; the register is `brand/licensed/assets.json`, the rules are in the channel folder's `brand/licensed/README.md`): the owner downloads each asset by hand from wherever it is licensed (WAV for music and effects if offered) and safelists the channel with the source if it has a safelist (Uppbeat: Account, Safelist); `video licensed add <file> --type music|sfx|stock-video|motion-graphic|lut --title ... --source <name> --plan <name> --channels <slug>` records it (copies it into `brand/licensed/<type>/`, sha256, refuses a missing file or a duplicate id; a LUT must be a real .cube); `video licensed list|check|channels|credits|remove`. A video uses assets by id. **Music:** `"music": [{"asset": id, "from": scene id or seconds, "fade": s, "gain_db": -18}]` (a string path in `"music"` still works for the owner's own file); each track starts at `from`, fades in and out, cross-fades into the next and loops if short; they are mixed and ducked under the voice with the same sidechain compression as any music. **Grade:** `"look": {"lut": id, "strength": 0 to 1}` runs the joined picture through the .cube once (`lut3d`, mixed back with the ungraded picture by strength); this is the only step that encodes the whole picture again. **Stock video scene:** `{"clip": {"asset": id, "start": s, "duration": s}, "narration": "..."}` has no template: the clip is scaled and cropped to fill the frame, silent, held on its last frame if short, encoded like every scene and joined by the usual pipeline; its time comes from its narration. **Overlay on any scene:** `"overlay": {"asset": id, "blend": "normal|screen", "opacity": 0 to 1, "at": s, "duration": s}` (a list works too) is composited after the scene render, `normal` keeps the alpha of a .mov or .webm, `screen` drops a black background (light leaks, dust). Changing a clip, overlay or the recorded file redraws only that scene. `video check` notes a missing LUT or clip file before the render stops. The vertical version carries none of it (a scene's `"vertical": {"clip": null, "template": ...}` replaces a clip scene). Licensed video and overlays decorate; they never carry the story: the explanation stays in the original scenes and the voice.
    **Generated shots** (an AI-generated clip or still, any provider; Higgsfield is the one we suggest, none is required; `/generated-shots` does it): record the finished file with `video licensed add <file> --type generated-video|generated-image --tool "<the tool>" [--prompt "..."]`, then use it by id like a stock clip or an overlay. Rules: the video must show a label on screen (default "Re-creation" for a generated video, "AI-generated" for an image; `--onscreen-label "text"` changes it, `--no-label "reason"` records why none is needed) and the final approval refuses while the label is not in the on-screen text; a generated asset is YouTube only unless it was recorded with `--vertical-ok` (then the vertical version may use it; licensed material never); it never carries the story. The rules are in `brand/licensed/README.md`.
    **Your own clips and overlays (Blender, code)**: a clip or overlay can be the owner's own render instead of a recorded asset: `{"clip": {"file": "assets/blender/product.mp4", "start": 0, "duration": 6, "mute": true}, "narration": "..."}` or `"overlay": {"file": "assets/blender/title.mov", "blend": "normal", "opacity": 1, "at": 1, "duration": 4}`. Exactly one of `"asset"` (licensed, by id) or `"file"` per clip or overlay. The path is relative to the video folder (or to the channel's `brand/library/`), must exist, and must stay inside the channel folder (an absolute path, `..` or a link that leads out is refused). Same behaviour as the licensed versions: a clip fills the frame (scaled and cropped, always muted, its last frame held), an overlay keeps its alpha (`.mov` ProRes 4444, `.webm`) or uses `"blend": "screen"`. The file's sha256 goes into the scene's cache hash, so a re-rendered file redraws only its own scene. It is the owner's own work: no register entry, no credit, `video licensed check` ignores it, and `video check` reports a missing file as a warning before the render stops. Own files ARE allowed in the vertical version (only licensed clips and overlays are refused there). `/blender-shot` and `brand/library/3d/` in the channel folder make Blender shots (optional, free; `video doctor` says whether Blender is found; the optional Blender Lab MCP is a faster live path, never required); `terrain.py` there makes shaded-relief maps from free elevation tiles, the one place the kit uses the network, only on its own command, and writes the attribution to put in the description.
    **Vertical for Instagram** (Susanoo Studio rule, 2026-11-13: Instagram always gets the full episode vertical, never widescreen with black bars): `video check <folder> --vertical` then `video render <folder> --vertical` draw the same scenes, voice and words at 1080x1920 with the Reel Kit's safe area (top 250, bottom 300, left 60, right 120: the Reel Kit's one safe zone, the app's buttons sit on the right) in `build/scenes-vertical/`, and write `out/vertical.mp4`. The wide video's scenes and caches are not touched. Look at the check sheets: the styles lay out for both shapes, but a scene sized for wide can sit near the edges.
11. **Thumbnail.** `video thumbnail <folder> [--at seconds | --scene n] --text "2 TO 5 *WORDS*"` makes `out/thumbnail.jpg` (1280x720): the frame with the words over it in heavy capitals, the `*word*` in the accent colour (`--side right` puts them on the right; `--plain`, or video.json `"thumbnail": {"plain": true}`, draws every word white with no accent colour, for styles where one coloured word looks wrong). **Your own picture as the base:** `--image <file>` (a Blender still, any image inside the channel folder; a path outside it is refused) replaces the scene frame, cropped to 1280x720 (`--fit cover`, the default, fills and crops; `--fit contain` shows the whole picture; a transparent picture is laid on `--bg`, default near black) with the same text treatment; it needs no voice or scenes. Make the approved idea, look at it full size and at 320 px wide. For YouTube's thumbnail test make versions with `--name a`, `--name b` (in `out/thumbnails/`); `--use a` makes one the thumbnail that is uploaded. `/thumbnail-design` takes a brief into an outside design tool (Canva, Adobe or any editor) or a Blender still, saves the picture inside the channel folder, puts it on the video with `--image`, and sets up the test. **Check it:** `video thumbcheck <folder | thumbnail.jpg> [--no-ocr] [--min-h 60] [--lines lines.json]` (Python 3, ffmpeg) tests facts from the pixels: file type and size (2 MB), 1280x720, text inside the safe margin and clear of YouTube's duration badge, the biggest line tall enough, not near-black or near-white, and still readable at 168x94; with no text reader (Apple Vision on a Mac, or Tesseract) the text is estimated and never fails. Fix what it flags before the upload.
12. **YouTube text.** Write `out/youtube.txt`: the title (100 characters at most, no < or >) on line 1, then the description (the promise, what the viewer learns, the call to action, no invented claims). Chapters are appended from `out/chapters.txt`, and the sources from `research.md` ("Sources:", its `## Sources` list) unless the description already names them or video.json says `"description_sources": false`. Also write `out/pinned-comment.txt`: one comment for the channel to pin (a question that starts the conversation, or the one thing the video left out, and the call to action; no invented claims); it is approved and fact-checked with the rest, the upload shows it, and the person posts and pins it by hand (the kit never posts comments). If the video uses licensed assets, run `video licensed credits <folder>` after writing the text: it adds a "Music and assets" block (each asset's credit text, or its title, artist and Uppbeat link) at the end of `out/youtube.txt`; run it before the final fact check, since it changes that file. Then `video captions <folder>` writes `out/captions.<lang>.srt` from the word clock (2 lines of 42 characters at most; language from video.json `"language"`, else `youtube.language` in brand.json, else en); make it again after any new voice (the upload refuses captions older than the voice). Optional in video.json: `"tags"` (500 characters in all), `"language"`, and `"publish_at"` (a time with its offset; the video uploads private and YouTube publishes it then; only on the person's say-so, like any privacy change).
13. **Final checks, then the person's yes.** `video qa <folder>` checks the finished file: 1920x1080 at 30 fps, as long as the voice (plus the `end_card`), -15 to -13 LUFS, true peak at most -1 dBTP, no black stretch over 0.5 s, nothing still for 4 s (2 s is a warning), a real voice. Fix what fails and render again. **Captions and mix:** `video captionscheck <folder | file.srt> [--words] [--lang en] [--max-cps 17] [--json]` checks reading speed (17 characters a second), line length (42) and two lines at most, and flashes under a second (`--words` checks the captions the kit would make from the word clock); `video mixcheck <folder | video.mp4> [--json]` (Python 3, ffmpeg) checks the finished mix: loudness, loudness range, clipping, long silences, the music dipping under the voice, and each chapter's level (`build/mix.json`). Fix FAILs and look at WARNs before the final approval. `video facts <folder> --scope final` checks the words again with the YouTube text added; re-read and `record --scope final --result PASS`. Then show the person the video, the thumbnail, the YouTube text and the pinned comment, and ask them to watch the whole final cut, start to end; then ask with AskUserQuestion: "I watched all of it and approve (Recommended)", "Change something", "Not yet". Only on "I watched all of it and approve": `video approve <folder> final --watched` (it needs the QA PASS, the final fact check and, when the video uses licensed assets, a passing `video licensed check` with the credits block in `out/youtube.txt`; it records those assets with their credits, and it fingerprints the files). Without `--watched` the approval is recorded but the upload refuses; never add `--watched` for someone who only saw part of it.
14. **Upload.** `video youtube login` once (Google Cloud "Desktop app" client; the Video Kit's SETUP.md section 5, which publishes the app before the first sign-in). It asks to upload and to manage the channel (captions need the second); `--upload-only` asks to upload only, and then captions are skipped. A sign-in made before captions existed needs `video youtube login` again for them. `BAGELUNIT_NO_BROWSER=1` prints the sign-in address instead of opening it, so it can be opened in the browser profile signed in to the channel's account. Then `video youtube upload <folder>` is a dry run that lists every problem, including a missing final approval or a file changed since it; `--yes` sends it, private by default. Never make a video public without the person saying so. Never upload twice (the kit refuses without `--again`). After the upload it sets the thumbnail, adds every `out/captions.<lang>.srt` and prints the pinned comment to post. Captions for a video already up: `video youtube captions <folder>` (a file already added unchanged is skipped).
15. **Reels from the video** (in a brand folder). `video reels <folder>` lists stretches of 20 to 50 seconds that could stand alone, chapter by chapter. Pick the strongest (an idea that stands on its own, with a number or a turn in it) and suggest them to the person with AskUserQuestion (the strongest stretches as multiSelect options, plus "None of these"); on their pick, `video reels <folder> --pick 3-5,12-14` shows what it would make and `--apply` makes Reel Kit reel folders in `reels/` (same style, drawn vertical, same scenes and words, `from_video` in reel.json, and `facts-from-video.md`: the research lines its numbers rest on, which the owner clears into `brand/facts.md`). Then `/bagelunit-reels` takes each one on: a new first line, its own ending pointing to the full video, its own voice at reel pace, gates A, B and C.
16. **Sources and archive.** Before the upload, `video sources <folder>` checks every DOI in research.md, script.txt, outline.md and the description at Crossref (free, no key; DataCite when Crossref does not know it) and lists each one's real title, first author and year: read the list against the claim each DOI sits on, since a wrong DOI pasted on the right claim still resolves. A DOI that does not resolve fails (exit 1). It also sends each public link in research.md and the description to the Wayback Machine (Save Page Now when there is no snapshot from the last 30 days; `--no-archive` skips it; it is slow on purpose, about 5 s a link) and writes `source-safety.md` and `.json`. After the final render, `video archive <folder> --to <rclone remote:path>` (or `"archive": {"to": ...}` in brand.json, or `BAGELUNIT_ARCHIVE_TO`) copies out/, the text and the paid voice with rclone and verifies by checksum; the person sets the remote up once with `rclone config`.
17. **Tidy.** Once it is uploaded and archived, `video tidy <folder>` lists the scene clips and joined copies that a render can make again (most of the folder's size); `--apply` deletes them. The words, research, plan, voice, approvals and out/ stay. It also says what that costs later: the next render of each shape draws every scene again (nothing is left to reuse), with an estimate from the folder's own render times (`build/render-times.json`, which tidy keeps; a 64-scene vertical episode took about 13 minutes). It warns, without blocking, when the episode has no verified archive: tidy only removes scratch a render makes again, but then the paid voice and out/ would exist in one place; say so and offer `video archive`. So tidy after the upload, not while a scene may still change.

## Channel review

`video review <channel folder> <export.csv ...>` reads YouTube Studio's own CSV exports and writes `reviews/YYYY-MM-DD.md` in the channel folder. Use it when the person asks how the channel or a video is doing, what to change, or before planning the next video of a channel that has a few uploads.

- **Real exports only.** Never type, guess or estimate a number. Without a file, the command prints exactly what to download (YouTube Studio, Analytics, Advanced mode, Content, the metrics, Export current view, CSV, unzip) and stops: tell the person that, or do it with them in the browser (they are logged in; you click through Studio, they download). It uses no YouTube sign-in.
- **What it reads:** the per-video table ("Table data.csv": views, impressions, impressions CTR, average view duration, average percentage viewed, watch time, duration, publish time; other column names for the same thing are read too) and, optionally, a video's audience retention export. A missing column is named in the review, never filled in. A zip or a day-by-day chart is refused with what to give instead.
- **What it writes:** what to keep in mind first (fewer than 5 videos, videos younger than 7 days, missing columns), then the observations (each number with the video it comes from, matched to `videos/<name>` by the YouTube id in `out/youtube-published.json` or the title), then hypotheses (labelled as not proven), then up to 5 tests for the next video (title, thumbnail, hook, length, topic), one change each, with the number to read afterwards.
- **Retention:** give a video's audience retention export too (Advanced mode, that video, Audience retention, Export; put the video's folder name or YouTube id in the file name, for example `moon.csv`). The review then has a "Retention (where viewers leave)" section: the first 30 seconds, average view duration against length, the steepest falls and the scene and chapter each sits in (from that video's `build/scene_times.json`, scenes.json and `out/chapters.txt`), hypotheses (not proven) and one to three tests. Without an export it says nothing about where viewers leave; it never guesses a curve.
- **A thumbnail or title test:** `video abtest --a <clicks> <impressions> --b <clicks> <impressions> [--c ...] [--min-impressions 1000] [--metric thumbnail] [--json]` (Python 3) takes YouTube Studio's own counts and says whether one variant is ahead enough to call (95 percent or more against each other variant, and enough impressions each), or how many more impressions to wait for. It never invents a number; clicks are one question, watch time is another.
- **Reading it to the person:** lead with the headline, say plainly when the sample is small, keep observations and guesses apart, and offer the tests as a choice (AskUserQuestion). The dashboard links the newest review.

## The dashboard

`video dashboard` draws the video dashboard: what needs the person, the numbers (in progress, uploaded, minutes, voice characters left, disk free), the work order with how many videos wait at each step, every video with its next command, a calendar (`"publish_on": "YYYY-MM-DD"` in video.json plans a date) and a card for each video that plays it. In a brand folder it is `videos/video-dashboard.html`, else `video-dashboard.html` in the folder given. Once it exists, every command that changes a video redraws it. `--credits` reads the ElevenLabs characters left (a free read of the person's own account; only when asked); `--open` opens it. Offer it after the first video is started, and point to it when the person asks what is going on.

## Long videos

There is no length limit: the kit has planned an 80-minute video in under a second and renders scene by scene, so a long video only takes longer. Splitting into parts is a suggestion, never a rule; the person decides.

- **When to suggest parts:** when the map runs over about 20 minutes, or has two or more halves that each stand on their own. Say why: more viewers finish a shorter video, each part is its own upload that can point to the next, and a fix re-renders one part instead of the whole thing. Ask with AskUserQuestion ("Split into parts (Recommended)", "One long video"); if they split, propose it: "Part 1: <title>" and so on, 10 to 20 minutes each, each with its own hook, promise and payoff, and one line at the start of Part 2 onward saying what came before. If the person wants one long video, make one long video.
- **Each part is its own folder** (`video new` per part, the same style, a copy of the same research.md), so each has its own voice, chapters, thumbnail and upload.
- **What a long video costs** (measured on a recent Mac): rendering takes about as long as the video itself (4.6 minutes rendered in 4.3); the folder needs about 65 MB of disk for each minute while it is built (an hour is about 4 GB, so check the free space first); the voice is about 900 characters a minute (an hour is about 55,000 characters, so check the person's ElevenLabs allowance before voicing).
- **YouTube:** an account that is not verified can only upload up to 15 minutes. Verifying it once (youtube.com/verify, with a phone number) allows up to 12 hours. Say so before a long upload.

## Rules

- Never type scene times; they come from the voice. Never edit `styles/` (copies: edit in the bagelunit-styles repo and sync again).
- The voice key is the person's: never ask for it, print it or store it.
- Everything on screen is original: no real people, no other companies' logos or characters, no outside footage or music. The outside material allowed is licensed material from any source you have rights to (Uppbeat is what we use and suggest) and generated shots (step 10), recorded in `brand/licensed/assets.json` and used by id (step 10); the channel must be safelisted with its source before a YouTube upload (if the source has a safelist), and `video approve final` refuses while `video licensed check` fails. A music file of the person's own can still go in `"music": "file"`.
- Anything scored must reach 90 or more; below that, redo and rescore. The voice does not start for a script without a recorded review of 90 or more for its exact words (the owner alone may skip it, with a reason).
- Report what actually happened: a render with errors is not done; a stand-in voice is not a voice.
- No em dashes in anything written.
